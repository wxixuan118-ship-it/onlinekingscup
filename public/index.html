The Classic Party Card Game

What Is Kings Cup?

Kings Cup is a social card game in which players draw from a shuffled deck and follow a different rule for each card rank. It is also known as Ring of Fire, Circle of Death, and Waterfall in different regions. The game is easy to start because there is no score sheet or complicated board: all you need is a standard deck, a group of players, and an agreed set of card meanings. Alcohol is optional, and every drinking instruction can be replaced with points, trivia, mocktails, or short challenges.

Players take turns drawing one card at a time. Number cards usually trigger quick actions such as Floor, Heaven, Rhyme, Mate, or Categories, while face cards can create longer roles such as Question Master. The Kings build toward the game's finale: traditionally, the first three King drawers contribute to a center cup and the fourth King drawer completes the agreed final action. Before starting, the group should review the Kings Cup rules and choose safe alternatives that work for everyone.

How to Play

Set up the deck, choose a turn order, draw clockwise, and complete each card's action before the next turn. See the full how-to-play guide.

Pro Tips

Kings Cup Tips for a Great Game

Set rules before you start Agree on any house rules before the first card — like whether the Mate rule stacks, or what "drinking" means in your group (sips vs. gulps).
Track the King's Cup carefully Keep an eye on the crown counter at the top of the screen. As the 4th King approaches, tension builds — that's the best part of the game!
Use Reshuffle between rounds The in-game menu has a Reshuffle option so you can keep the same version and reset the deck without going back to the start screen.
Best played on a shared screen Place your phone in the center of the table on a stand, or cast it to a TV. Everyone can see the card and rule without crowding around.
Non-alcoholic is just as fun The game works perfectly with juice, sparkling water, or any beverage. The Family version replaces all drinking rules with physical challenges entirely.
