What Is the King's Cup Drinking Game?

King's Cup is a social card game played with a standard deck. It is also known as Ring of Fire, Circle of Death, and Waterfall in different regions. Players sit around the deck, draw clockwise, and follow a quick rule connected to the card they reveal.

The game does not use scores in its traditional form. Its structure comes from reaction challenges, word games, temporary roles, and the four Kings. Alcohol is not required: every drinking action can be replaced with points, trivia, a non-alcoholic sip, or a harmless challenge.

Setup in Brief

Seat three to ten players (four to eight plays best) around a table, put a center cup in the middle, and spread a shuffled 52-card deck face down around it. Everyone keeps their own drink within reach. Agree on the card meanings and the ending before the first draw — the full table setup is in the how to play walkthrough and the setup guide.

Who Drinks, and When

Draw one card, read the action aloud, and resolve the drink before the next person draws. Most ranks send a sip somewhere:

  • Nominate cards (2 You, 3 Me): the drawer sends a drink to another player or takes one.
  • Loser-drinks challenges (4 Floor, 7 Heaven, 9 Rhyme, 10 Categories): whoever reacts slowest or slips up drinks.
  • Role cards (Thumb Master, Question Master, Mate): the role lets its holder make others drink until it passes to someone else.
  • Waterfall (Ace): everyone drinks at once and can only stop after the person before them stops.

Exact ranks differ by group, so set the sheet with the complete King's Cup rules before you start.

Drink pacing: keep sips small, alternate with water, put out food, and let anyone swap in a soft drink for any card. Never enforce a rule that pressures someone to keep up.

What Happens With the Four Kings?

  1. First King: add a small contribution to the center cup.
  2. Second King: add another small contribution.
  3. Third King: add the third contribution.
  4. Fourth King: complete the finale agreed before the game.

Never create an unsafe mixture or pressure anyone to consume it. A challenge bowl, final trivia question, or collecting the letters K-I-N-G gives the same four-card suspense without a mixed drink. See the focused King card rule for common variations.

Safer and Non-Drinking Options

  • Use small sips of water, soda, juice, or another chosen beverage.
  • Give one penalty point for losing a challenge.
  • Let every player use a no-explanation-needed pass.
  • Replace the center cup with written prompts.
  • Never combine substances or encourage rapid consumption.
  • Stop immediately if the game is no longer comfortable for someone.

For a complete replacement system, use the King's Cup without alcohol guide.

King’s Cup Drinking Game FAQ

What happens when you draw a King in King's Cup?

Traditionally, the first three King drawers add a small amount to the center cup, and the fourth King drawer completes the agreed finale.

Can you play King's Cup without alcohol?

Yes. Replace drinking actions with points, trivia, non-alcoholic drinks, or short challenges.
