Is There a Kings Cup Venue Near Me?

No, and that is the point. Kings Cup is not a bar game, an arcade or an event you buy tickets to. It is a card game played at home, at pre-drinks, at house parties and on quiet nights in. There is no chain, no franchise and no location to search for — the game is wherever the people with the deck are.

If you searched hoping for a pub that hosts it, you will usually be disappointed. A few bars run card-game nights, but none is built around Kings Cup specifically. The game is meant to be played with your own group, on your own table, with your own rules.

Play Kings Cup Near You at Home

To play Kings Cup near you at home, you need very little. A standard 52-card deck, a large cup for the centre of the table, and a drink for each player (or soft drinks and a challenge bowl for the non-drinking version). Three to ten players is the sweet spot, and four to eight plays best.

Spread the shuffled deck face down in a ring around the cup, agree on what each card means, then draw one card each clockwise. The Kings Cup rules page has every card meaning and the full setup, and the setup guide covers deck layout, seating and how to agree the rules before the first draw.

Play Kings Cup Online With Friends

If you do not have a physical deck, or your group is spread across different places, you can play Kings Cup near you online. The online deck shuffles itself, removes drawn cards and shows the rule after every draw, so nobody has to remember the list. One host can share their screen on a video call and pause after each card, or each player can open the same page and draw together.

The online version uses the same card meanings as the physical game, so the rules you agreed before play still apply. The Kings Cup app page has more on playing from a phone, and the without alcohol guide covers swapping drinks for points if your group would rather play sober.

Set Up Kings Cup Near You in Five Minutes

Kings Cup takes about five minutes to set up, which is why it works as a “near me” game: you do not travel to it, it travels with you. Seat everyone around a stable surface, put the cup in the middle, spread the deck face down in a ring, agree the card meanings, and start drawing.

The only thing that can slow you down is a mid-game argument about what 5, 6 or Jack means. Agree those three cards before the first draw and the rest of the game runs itself. Wikipedia catalogues the game and its many names under King game if you want the regional history.
