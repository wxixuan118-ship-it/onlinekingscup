Kings Cup Rules Variations

The same deck, a dozen different tables. Here are the Kings Cup rules variations people actually play — card swaps, house rules and whole-game formats, with when to use each.

Kings Cup Online editors · Updated October 5, 2026

Kings Cup rules variations in one sentence: keep the draw-and-follow loop, then swap what one or two cards mean or change how the game is structured — agree every change before the first card is drawn so nobody argues at 1am.

What Are Kings Cup Rules Variations?

These variations are the changes a group agrees to before the first draw. They come in two flavours: a single card that does something different than the default list, or a change to the whole shape of the game. Both keep the same core loop — draw one card clockwise, do what the rank says, pour into the cup on the first three Kings and drink it on the fourth. Wikipedia catalogues the game and its many names under King game. The full Kings Cup rules explain the default loop; this page lists the swaps people actually use.

No variation is official. Kings Cup has no governing body, which is exactly why every student house and every friend group plays a slightly different version. The only rule that matters is that the table agrees before play starts. Write the swaps down or say them out loud, then carry on.

Card Swap Variations

These are the single-card swaps that turn up most often. Each one replaces the default meaning of one rank with something else. Change only one or two at a time so the game stays easy to follow.

5 as Guys or Thumb Master

The default 5 is Thumb Master, where the drawer can drop a thumb on the table at any moment and the last player to copy drinks. The older and still very common swap is 5 as Guys, where every man at the table drinks. Thumb Master works at any table; Guys only works when the group is comfortable with a gender split. The 5 rule page covers both.

6 as Chicks or Challenge

The default 6 is a group Challenge agreed before play. The rhyming swap is 6 as Chicks, where every woman drinks. As with 5, the Challenge version is inclusive and the Chicks version is the classic. Agree which one you are using before the first draw.

Jack as Never Have I Ever or Make a Rule

The default Jack is Never Have I Ever. The most popular swap for this card is Jack as Make a Rule, where the drawer invents one simple rule that lasts until it is replaced. Make a Rule produces the most chaotic games, which is why people remember it. See the Jack rule page for both.

Ace as Snake Eyes

Instead of Waterfall, Ace becomes Snake Eyes: nobody may make eye contact with the player who drew it until the next Ace. Anyone caught looking drinks. It is slower than Waterfall but produces the same tension.

2 as Two for You

The default 2 is You, where the drawer picks one player to drink. The swap is Two for You, where the drawer splits two drinks between two players instead of sending both to one person. It spreads the action around a larger table.

Game Format Variations

These changes affect the whole structure of the game rather than a single card. They are useful when the default setup does not fit the group size, the setting or whether alcohol is involved.

Short game

Use half a deck or set a 20-minute timer. The last King drawn before time runs out triggers the finale. This short-format setup keeps a pre-drinks game from eating the whole evening.

Large group

With ten or more players, split into teams and cap word games at five seconds so turns keep moving. Reaction cards (Floor, Heaven) stay the same; nomination cards go to one team instead of one player.

Two players or couples

Replace the group-dependent cards (Floor, Heaven, Mate) with shared prompts and keep the reaction rounds between the two of you. The Kings Cup for two guide has the full swap list.

Remote

One host draws from the online deck on a video call and pauses after each card. Pick camera-friendly actions: Thumb Master becomes a thumbs-up to camera, Question Master works over voice, and Waterfall is everyone drinking together on screen.

Points

One penalty point per lost challenge instead of a drink; the lowest total wins when the deck runs out. This is the most common non-drinking version and the without alcohol guide covers the full substitution list.

Challenge bowl

Each King adds a written dare to the center cup instead of a drink. Whoever draws the fourth King pulls one dare out for the group. It keeps the four-King suspense with no mixing of drinks.

Choosing Kings Cup Rules Variations for Your Group

Not every swap fits every table. A few rules of thumb: change only a few elements at once so turns stay fast; prefer inclusive cards (Thumb Master, Challenge) over gendered ones (Guys, Chicks) at mixed tables; and for sober or family groups, swap every drink for a point before you start. Kings Cup Rules Variations FAQ

What are Kings Cup rules variations?

They are the agreed changes a group makes to the standard card list or game format before play — card swaps like Jack as Make a Rule, or formats like short games and points-based play.

What is the most common Kings Cup variation?

The most common swap is the card swap: 5 as Guys, 6 as Chicks and Jack as Make a Rule. Many modern tables play 5 as Thumb Master and 6 as Challenge instead.

Can you play Kings Cup variations without alcohol?

Yes. Every drink in any version can be swapped for a penalty point, water, a trivia question or a dare, and the center cup becomes a challenge bowl.
