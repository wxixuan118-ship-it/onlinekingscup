Kings Cup Rules at a Glance

  1. Place a central cup on the table and arrange a shuffled deck face down around it.
  2. Players draw one card at a time in clockwise order.
  3. Each card rank has an action. Complete it before the next turn.
  4. Each King advances the Kings Cup finale.
  5. The classic round ends on the fourth King; your group may instead finish the deck.
Before you begin: agree on card meanings, opt-outs, the ending, and non-drinking substitutions. Alcohol is never required.

Every Kings Cup Card Rule

Ace — Waterfall

Everyone starts the agreed action together. The drawer may stop first, then each player in clockwise order may stop only after the person before them. For a sober version, hold a pose or perform a simple movement instead.

Two — You

The drawer chooses another player to take the agreed action. A friendly alternative is to award that player a point or ask them a light question.

Three — Me

The person who drew the card takes the action. This is the simplest rule to remember: three means me.

Four — Floor

Everyone tries to touch the floor. The last person to do it takes the action. Make sure the play area is clear and nobody has to move dangerously.

Five — Thumb Master

The drawer becomes Thumb Master. At any later moment, they quietly place a thumb on the table; everyone copies, and the last player takes the action. The role lasts until another Five appears. Some groups use a group-based drinking rule here, but Thumb Master is more inclusive.

Six — Choose a Challenge

The drawer chooses a short, safe group challenge. Examples include making an animal sound, naming a favorite movie, or holding a funny pose. Decide a fixed Six rule before the game if your group prefers faster turns.

Seven — Heaven

Everyone points upward. The last person to raise a hand takes the action. Seven rhymes with heaven, making the rule easy for beginners to recall.

Eight — Mate

Choose another player as your mate. Whenever either partner takes a game action, the other joins. The partnership usually lasts until the next Eight is drawn.

Nine — Rhyme

The drawer says a word. Moving clockwise, each player gives a new rhyme. The first person who pauses too long, repeats a word, or gives an invalid rhyme takes the action. Choose words with several possible rhymes to keep it fun.

Ten — Categories

The drawer names a category such as breakfast foods, cities, or movie characters. Players take turns naming unique examples. The first repeat, incorrect answer, or timeout ends the round.

Jack — Never Have I Ever

The drawer says something they have never done. Players for whom the statement is true respond using the group's chosen action. Keep prompts respectful; nobody should have to reveal private information. Another common Jack rule is “Make a Rule.”

Queen — Question Master

The drawer becomes Question Master. If another player answers one of their questions, that player takes the action. The role continues until the next Queen. Statements and rhetorical questions can be excluded if your group wants a fairer version.

King — Kings Cup

In the traditional version, each of the first three King drawers contributes a small amount to the central cup. The fourth King drawer faces the agreed finale. A safer alternative is to add written challenges to a bowl, earn letters in a word, or take a final trivia question. Never use a mixture that could be unsafe.

Optional Joker Rules

Jokers are not required. If included, use them as wild cards: invent a new rule, swap seats, reverse play direction, or let the drawer remove an existing rule. State the Joker rule before shuffling.

House Rules That Work Well

  • Five-second limit: keep Rhyme and Categories moving with a short countdown.
  • No repeats: an answer used earlier in the same round cannot be reused.
  • Rule cap: allow no more than three persistent custom rules at once.
  • Broken circle: traditionally, the person who breaks the ring takes an action; skip this online.
  • Universal pass: every player gets one no-explanation-needed pass per game.
