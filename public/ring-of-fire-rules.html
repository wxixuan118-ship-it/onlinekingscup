What Is Ring of Fire?

Ring of Fire is a drinking card game where players sit in a circle, spread a deck of cards face-down around a central cup, and take turns drawing one card at a time. Each card triggers a different rule — from drinking challenges to social games. The player who draws the 4th King must drink the entire central cup.

It's also known by several other names depending on where you play:

Ring of Fire 🔥 Kings Cup 👑 Circle of Death 💀 King's Game Waterfall

Ring of Fire is the dominant name in the UK, Ireland, and Australia. In the United States, the same game is usually called Kings Cup. The rules are identical — only the name changes.

What You Need

  • 3–10 players (best with 4–8)
  • A standard 52-card deck (add Jokers for extra wild rules)
  • One large central cup (the "Ring of Fire" cup)
  • Drinks for each player — alcoholic or not

Setup — How to Play Ring of Fire

  1. Sit in a circle around a table.
  2. Place an empty cup in the centre of the table — this is the Ring of Fire cup.
  3. Spread the full deck face-down in a ring around the cup. Cards should form a complete circle — don't leave gaps.
  4. Decide who goes first (youngest player, or a quick coin flip).
  5. Players take turns drawing one card at a time, clockwise, and following the rule for that card.
  6. The game ends when all four Kings have been drawn.

⚠️ The Ring Rule: If you break the ring of cards when drawing, you must drink immediately. This keeps everyone careful.

Ten — Categories

Name a category (e.g. "Premier League clubs"). Go around the circle — each player names one item. First person who repeats or blanks drinks.

Jack — Make a Rule

Invent any new rule that applies for the rest of the game. Breaking the rule means drinking. Popular examples: "no saying names," "drink with your left hand only."

Queen — Question Master

You are now the Question Master. Any player who answers a question you ask must drink. Lasts until another Queen is drawn.

King — Pour into the Cup

Pour some of your drink into the central Ring of Fire cup. The player who draws the 4th and final King must drink the entire cup.

🃏 Playing with Jokers? Optional but fun. Common Joker rules: everyone drinks, or the drawer must invent an outrageous new rule on the spot.

Ring of Fire vs Kings Cup — What's the Difference?

Same game, same core loop. "Ring of Fire" is the common name in the UK, Ireland and Australia; "Kings Cup" is used in the US and Canada; "Circle of Death" is the US college name. A few card actions (5, 6, Jack, Ace), the broken-circle penalty and the fourth-King finale change by house rules — not by which name you use.

Popular Ring of Fire Variations

House rules are a huge part of Ring of Fire's appeal. Here are the most popular card rule swaps:

  • Jack — Never Have I Ever: Each player puts up 3 fingers. Go around saying "Never have I ever…" — put a finger down if you have. Last with fingers up wins; others drink.
  • Two — New Rule: Instead of nominating someone to drink, the drawer creates a new game rule.
  • Four — Thumb Master: The drawer secretly puts their thumb on the table at any time. Last player to notice and copy drinks.
  • Nine — Bust a Rhyme: Same as standard, but you must rap your rhyme.
  • Ace — Social: Everyone drinks at the same time, no waterfall needed.

Tips for Hosting Ring of Fire

  • Use a big cup — the Ring of Fire cup fills up fast once Kings start appearing. A pint glass or large cup works well.
  • Agree on house rules before starting — avoids arguments when the Jack is drawn.
  • Non-alcoholic play is great — use juice, sparkling water, or any soft drink. All the fun, none of the hangover.
  • Keep cards spread evenly — if the ring breaks into sections, re-spread them before continuing.
  • Print a rule card — or use our online version which shows each card's rule automatically as you draw.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Ring of Fire the same as Kings Cup?

Yes — same game, same core loop, different regional names. Ring of Fire is the common UK name; Kings Cup is used in the US; Circle of Death is the US college name. A few card actions change by house rules, not by which name you use. See Kings Cup vs Ring of Fire for the full comparison.

How many people do you need to play Ring of Fire?

Minimum 3 players, maximum around 10. The sweet spot is 4–8 players — enough for chaos but not so many the game drags.

What happens when you draw a King in Ring of Fire?

You pour some of your drink into the central cup. When the 4th King is drawn, that player must drink the entire cup — often a horrible mix of everyone's drinks.

Can you play Ring of Fire without alcohol?

Absolutely. Use any drink — juice, soda, water. Some groups replace drinking with physical forfeits (e.g. push-ups, silly dares). Our Family version is built specifically for non-alcoholic play.

What do you need to play Ring of Fire?

A standard 52-card deck, one large central cup, and drinks for each player. That's it — or play our free online version which provides everything digitally.

How long does a game of Ring of Fire last?

Usually 20–45 minutes depending on player count and how long rule challenges take. More players = longer game.
