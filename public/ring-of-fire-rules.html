What Is Ring of Fire?

Ring of Fire is a drinking card game where players sit in a circle, spread a deck of cards face-down around a central cup, and take turns drawing one card at a time. Each card triggers a different rule — from drinking challenges to social games. The player who draws the 4th King must drink the entire central cup.

It's also known by several other names depending on where you play:

Ring of Fire 🔥 Kings Cup 👑 Circle of Death 💀 King's Game Waterfall

Ring of Fire is the dominant name in the UK, Ireland, and Australia. In the United States, the same game is usually called Kings Cup. The rules are identical — only the name changes.

What You Need

  • 3–10 players (best with 4–8)
  • A standard 52-card deck (add Jokers for extra wild rules)
  • One large central cup (the "Ring of Fire" cup)
  • Drinks for each player — alcoholic or not

Setup — How to Play Ring of Fire

  1. Sit in a circle around a table.
  2. Place an empty cup in the centre of the table — this is the Ring of Fire cup.
  3. Spread the full deck face-down in a ring around the cup. Cards should form a complete circle — don't leave gaps.
  4. Decide who goes first (youngest player, or a quick coin flip).
  5. Players take turns drawing one card at a time, clockwise, and following the rule for that card.
  6. The game ends when all four Kings have been drawn.

Why It’s Called Ring of Fire — and How the Ring Works

The name comes from the ring of cards, not the cup. Kings Cup is named after the drink built in the centre; Ring of Fire is named after the burning circle of face-down cards that surrounds it and shrinks card by card as the game runs. That is why groups using this name are far more likely to treat the circle itself as part of the rules.

Spreading the ring

Shuffle the full deck and lay the cards face down in a single overlapping circle around the cup, working outward from one point until the loop closes. Aim for a continuous ring with no gaps — cards should touch or slightly overlap. A ring that is too tight makes clean draws impossible; too loose and the circle breaks constantly. With fewer than five players, spread a wider, looser circle so nobody has to lean across the table.

Breaking the ring

Breaking the ring means separating the circle while drawing — usually by dragging a neighbouring card out of place or opening a visible gap. This is the one rule that varies most between tables, so settle it out loud before the first draw:

House ruleWhat a break costsBest for
Strict ringWhoever broke it drinks immediately, then re-spreads the circleExperienced groups who want the extra tension
StandardForfeit only if a card is pushed fully clear of the ringMost groups — the usual default
RelaxedNo forfeit; just close the gap and carry onMixed-experience tables and crowded surfaces
PointsOne penalty point instead of a drinkNon-drinking and family games

Re-spreading mid-game

The circle naturally thins as cards are drawn, and by the halfway mark it usually stops looking like a ring at all. Pause and re-spread the remaining cards into a smaller circle rather than letting them drift into piles — a scattered deck kills the broken-ring rule and makes the last few draws awkward. Re-spreading is not a forfeit, and any player can call for it.

⚠️ Agree the ring rule first: the broken-ring penalty causes more mid-game arguments than any card. Pick a row from the table above before anyone draws.

Ring of Fire vs Kings Cup

Same game. The name tells you roughly where a group learned to play, not how they play — two tables using different names may work from an identical card sheet, or differ on ten cards. Popular Ring of Fire Variations

House rules are a huge part of Ring of Fire's appeal. Here are the most popular card rule swaps:

  • Jack — Never Have I Ever: Each player puts up 3 fingers. Go around saying "Never have I ever…" — put a finger down if you have. Last with fingers up wins; others drink.
  • Two — New Rule: Instead of nominating someone to drink, the drawer creates a new game rule.
  • Four — Thumb Master: The drawer secretly puts their thumb on the table at any time. Last player to notice and copy drinks.
  • Nine — Bust a Rhyme: Same as standard, but you must rap your rhyme.
  • Ace — Social: Everyone drinks at the same time, no waterfall needed.

Tips for Hosting Ring of Fire

  • Use a big cup — the Ring of Fire cup fills up fast once Kings start appearing. A pint glass or large cup works well.
  • Agree on house rules before starting — avoids arguments when the Jack is drawn.
  • Non-alcoholic play is great — use juice, sparkling water, or any soft drink. All the fun, none of the hangover.
  • Keep cards spread evenly — if the ring breaks into sections, re-spread them before continuing.
  • Print a rule card — or use our online version which shows each card's rule automatically as you draw.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is it called Ring of Fire?

The name comes from the ring of cards, not from the cup. The deck is spread face down in an unbroken circle around the centre cup, and that burning ring shrinks card by card as the game runs. Groups that enforce a penalty for breaking the circle are the reason the ring, rather than the King, ended up naming the game.

What happens if you break the ring in Ring of Fire?

It depends on the house rule agreed before the first draw. The strict version makes the player who broke it drink immediately and re-spread the circle. Softer versions only apply a forfeit when a card is pushed fully clear of the ring, and relaxed groups simply re-spread with no penalty at all. The ring rules table above lists the four common settings.

Do you have to use the 5 = Guys and 6 = Chicks rules?

No. They are traditional on UK sheets but entirely optional. The usual swaps are Thumb Master for 5 and Challenge for 6, which keep the same reaction-and-forfeit rhythm without splitting the table by gender. The gender-neutral rules sheet uses those by default.

What happens when you draw a King in Ring of Fire?

You pour some of your drink into the centre cup. The player who draws the fourth and final King finishes that cup, which ends the game. Groups that would rather not build a mixed drink use a challenge bowl or a final dare instead, keeping the same four-King suspense.

Is Ring of Fire the same as Kings Cup?

Yes, and the name does not tell you how a group plays. Ring of Fire is the usual name in the UK, Ireland and Australia; Kings Cup is used in North America. Two tables using different names may play from an identical card sheet, or differ on ten cards. See Kings Cup vs Ring of Fire for the full picture.

Can you play Ring of Fire without alcohol?

Yes. Swap every drink for a short physical forfeit or a penalty point and replace the centre cup with a challenge bowl. The reaction rounds, rhymes and categories are what drive the game, so nothing in the structure breaks. The sober rules guide has a full substitution system.

How long does a game of Ring of Fire last?

Usually 20 to 45 minutes. Player count and how long rule challenges run are what move that figure; more players means more turns between Kings and a longer game.
