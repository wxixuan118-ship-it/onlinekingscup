Ring of Fire Rules

Ring of Fire is the same game as Kings Cup, just named after the circle of cards. Here are the Ring of Fire rules people play in the UK, Ireland and Australia — every card, the broken ring penalty, and the fourth-King finale.

Ring of Fire rules in one sentence: spread the deck face down in a ring around a center cup, draw one card clockwise and do what its rank says, while the first three Kings pour into the cup and whoever draws the fourth King drinks it.

What Are the Ring of Fire Rules?

Ring of Fire is a drinking card game for 3–10 players. A shuffled 52-card deck is spread face down in an unbroken ring around a cup in the middle of the table. Players take turns clockwise, drawing one card and carrying out the action its rank assigns. The first three players to draw a King pour some of their drink into the center cup, and whoever draws the fourth King drinks it — which usually ends the game.

The name comes from the circle of face-down cards rather than the cup. Ring of Fire is the usual name in the UK, Ireland and Australia; in North America the same game is usually called Kings Cup, and Circle of Death is another common alias. The Kings Cup vs Ring of Fire guide compares the two names in full. Wikipedia lists the game and its aliases under King game.

Ring of Fire Rules by Card

These are the card meanings most commonly used under Ring of Fire rules. House rules vary most on 5, 6 and Jack, so agree those before the first draw. The full default list is also on the Kings Cup rules page.

Ace — Waterfall

Everyone starts drinking at the same time. The player who drew the Ace stops first; moving clockwise, each player may stop only after the person before them stops, so the last player drinks the longest.

2 rule — You

The drawer picks any player, and that player drinks. Some groups let the drawer split two drinks between two people instead.

3 rule — Me

The player who drew the card drinks. The rhyme “three is me” makes it the easiest to remember.

4 rule — Floor

Everyone touches the floor. The last one to get there drinks. Keep the area clear so nobody dives off a chair.

5 — Guys (or Thumb Master)

In many Ring of Fire tables, 5 means Guys: every man at the table drinks. Others play 5 as Thumb Master, where the drawer can drop a thumb on the table and the last player to copy drinks. The 5 rule page covers both.

6 — Chicks (or Challenge)

The rhyming pair to 5: 6 means Chicks, every woman drinks. The inclusive alternative is Challenge, where the drawer starts a short group challenge agreed before play.

7 rule — Heaven

Everyone points a hand at the sky the moment the card is flipped. The last hand up drinks.

8 rule — Mate

The drawer picks a mate. From then on, whenever one of them drinks, the other drinks too, until the next 8 replaces the pairing.

9 rule — Rhyme

The drawer says a word. Moving clockwise, each player has to rhyme. The first to repeat, miss the rhyme or stall drinks.

10 rule — Categories

The drawer names a category. Players go around naming items until someone repeats, gives an invalid answer or stalls. That player drinks.

Jack — Never Have I Ever (or Make a Rule)

The drawer says something they have never done, and everyone who has done it drinks. Many Ring of Fire tables play Jack as Make a Rule instead, where the drawer invents one rule that lasts until replaced. The Jack rule page has both.

Queen rule — Question Master

The drawer becomes Question Master until the next Queen. Anyone who answers one of their questions drinks; the only escape is to reply with a question of your own.

King — Ring of Fire

The first three Kings pour into the center cup. Whoever draws the fourth King drinks the whole cup and the game ends. Never mix spirits into an unsafe combination and never pressure anyone to finish it.

The Ring of Fire Ring-Break Rule

The one rule that really belongs to the Ring of Fire name is the penalty for breaking the circle of cards. Because the game is named after the ring, tables that use this name tend to treat the ring itself as part of the rules. Breaking the ring means opening a visible gap or dragging a neighbouring card out of place while drawing.

House ruleWhat a break costsBest for
Strict ringWhoever broke it drinks immediately, then re-spreads the circleExperienced groups who want the extra tension
StandardForfeit only if a card is pushed fully clear of the ringMost groups — the usual default
RelaxedNo forfeit; close the gap and carry onMixed-experience tables and crowded surfaces

Agree the ring rule before anyone draws. The broken-ring penalty causes more mid-game arguments than any card in the deck, so pick a row from the table above and stick to it.

Ring of Fire Rules Setup

Lay the cards face down in a single overlapping circle around the cup with no gaps. Too tight and clean draws are impossible; too loose and the circle breaks constantly. With fewer than five players, spread a wider ring so nobody has to lean across the table. The Kings Cup setup guide has the full checklist.

Before the first draw, agree on the card meanings, the rule for 5, 6 and Jack, how long an answer may take, the broken-ring penalty, and whether the game ends on the fourth King or after the whole deck.

Ring of Fire Rules FAQ

What are the Ring of Fire rules?

Ring of Fire is the same game as Kings Cup: spread a shuffled deck face down in a ring around a center cup, draw one card clockwise, and do what each rank says. The first three Kings pour into the cup and the fourth King drinks it.

What happens if you break the ring in Ring of Fire?

It depends on the house rule. A strict table makes whoever broke the circle drink immediately; the common default is a forfeit only when a card is pushed fully clear; relaxed tables just close the gap. Agree before the first draw.

Is Ring of Fire the same as Kings Cup?

Yes. Same game, same core loop. Ring of Fire is the name used mostly outside North America; a few card actions and the broken-circle penalty change by house rules, not by the name.
