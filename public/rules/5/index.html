Kings Cup Rules 5 Drive

The Kings Cup rules 5 drive variation is one of three common meanings for the 5 card, along with Thumb Master and Guys. Here is how each one plays at the table.

What Is 5 in Kings Cup Rules?

In Kings Cup rules, 5 is one of the most argued cards because it has three common meanings and no official answer. The table has to agree before the first draw which version it is using. The default in most modern lists is Thumb Master, the classic rhyming version is Guys, and a lively reaction-based swap is Drive.

Because 5 varies so much, it is one of the three cards (along with 6 and Jack) that every table should settle out loud before play. The full list lives on the Kings Cup rules page; this page is just the 5.

Kings Cup 5 as Thumb Master

Thumb Master is the most common modern meaning of the 5 card. The player who draws a 5 becomes Thumb Master until the next 5 is drawn. At any point in the game, the Thumb Master can quietly rest a thumb on the edge of the table. Every other player has to copy the gesture, and the last one to notice drinks.

The fun of Thumb Master is that it is an ambush, not a reaction to the card being flipped. A good Thumb Master drops the thumb mid-conversation, several turns after drawing the card, so people have let their guard down. The role lasts until another 5 appears and the new drawer takes over.

Kings Cup 5 as Guys

Guys is the older, rhyming version of the 5 rule. When a 5 is drawn, every man at the table drinks. It pairs with 6 as Chicks, where every woman drinks, which is why the two are usually played together.

Guys still turns up at tables that learned the game from an older list, but it does not work well for mixed-gender or non-binary groups, which is why Thumb Master has become the default. If your table wants the rhyming pair, play 5 as Guys and 6 as Chicks; otherwise play 5 as Thumb Master and 6 as Challenge.

Kings Cup Rules 5 Drive Variation

The Kings Cup rules 5 Drive variation is a reaction-based alternative to Thumb Master. When a player draws a 5, they mimic turning a steering wheel with both hands. Every other player has to copy the driving motion immediately. The last player to start, or the first one to stop before the drawer does, drinks.

In some versions of the Drive rule, the drawer can keep the steering-wheel motion going for several seconds and drop it without warning; anyone who stops too early or starts too late drinks. It works like Thumb Master but with a bigger, more visible gesture, so it is harder to miss and tends to produce louder reactions. The variations page lists Drive alongside other card swaps.

Which Kings Cup 5 Rule to Play

For most tables, Thumb Master is the best 5 rule: it works for any group, it creates a slow-burn ambush, and it pairs with 6 as Challenge. Play 5 as Guys if you want the classic rhyming pair with 6 as Chicks and your table is comfortable with a gender split. Play the Kings Cup rules 5 Drive variation if you want a bigger, more visible reaction card that is harder to forget.

Whatever you pick, say it out loud before the first card is drawn. The 5 argument is the most common mid-game dispute in Kings Cup, and it is entirely avoidable.

Kings Cup Rules 5 FAQ

What is 5 in Kings Cup rules?

5 is most commonly Thumb Master: the drawer can put a thumb on the table at any moment and the last player to copy drinks. Older tables play 5 as Guys, and some play 5 as Drive.

What is the Drive variation for 5?

In the Drive variation, the drawer mimics turning a steering wheel and the last player to copy (or the first to stop) drinks. It is a reaction-based alternative to Thumb Master.

Is 5 Guys or Thumb Master?

Both exist. Thumb Master is the modern default; Guys is the classic version where every man drinks. Agree which one your table is using before the first draw.
