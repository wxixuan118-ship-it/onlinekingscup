Kings Cup Rules Jack

The Kings Cup rules Jack card is one of the most varied in the deck: most tables play it as Never Have I Ever, but the Make a Rule variation is just as common. Here is how each one works.

What Is Jack in Kings Cup Rules?

In Kings Cup rules, Jack is the card most likely to start a table argument because it has no single agreed meaning. The two you will see most often are Never Have I Ever (the most common modern version) and Make a Rule (the classic house-rule version). The full list lives on the Kings Cup rules page; this page is just the Jack.

Because Jack changes from table to table, it is one of the three cards (along with 5 and 6) that every group should settle out loud before the first draw. Agreeing on the Jack rule before play avoids mid-game disputes.

Jack as Never Have I Ever

Never Have I Ever is the default meaning of Jack in most modern Kings Cup lists. The player who draws a Jack says a statement beginning with "Never have I ever…" followed by something they have never done. Every player at the table who has done that thing drinks.

The fun of Never Have I Ever is that it doubles as a getting-to-know-you mechanic. A good statement is specific enough to be interesting but general enough that someone at the table has done it. The drawer is not allowed to make something up just to watch everyone drink, and the rule works fine whether the statements are tame or silly.

Kings Cup Rules Jack as Make a Rule

Make a Rule is the classic alternate Jack rule. When a player draws a Jack, they get to create one simple table rule that stays in force until another Jack is drawn and a new rule replaces it. Typical rules are "drink with your left hand", "no first names", or "say cheers before every sip".

Make a Rule tends to compound over the course of a game: by the third Jack, three rules are stacked, and forgetting any of them means a drink. Keep the rules simple and reversible, and avoid anything that forces a player to do something they are uncomfortable with.

Which Jack Rule to Play

Play Jack as Never Have I Ever if you want a social, conversation-starting card that reveals small facts about the table. Play Jack as Make a Rule if you want a chaos card that keeps changing the game as it goes. Many tables mix the two by playing the first Jack as Never Have I Ever and letting the second Jack player choose.

Whatever you pick, say it out loud before the first card is drawn. The Jack argument is one of the most common mid-game disputes in Kings Cup, and it is entirely avoidable. The variations page lists the Jack swap alongside other card variations.
